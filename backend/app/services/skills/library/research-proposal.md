A proposal is judged on whether the method can actually answer the question with
the resources available. Most weak proposals have an interesting question and a
method that cannot reach it.

## Structure

1. **Title** — the variables and the setting, in one line.
2. **Background** — what is known, from the literature. Not a history of the
   field.
3. **Problem statement** — the gap, stated as a claim. See the
   `literature-review` skill; the gap comes out of the evidence, not out of "no
   study has been done in this district".
4. **Objectives** — one general, three to five specific. Each specific objective
   must be answerable by one part of the method. If it is not, one of them is
   wrong.
5. **Research questions / hypotheses** — matched one-to-one with the objectives.
6. **Methodology** — the section that gets you passed or sent back.
7. **Ethics**.
8. **Work plan and budget**.
9. **References**.

## The methodology section

State and JUSTIFY each of these — a justification is why this choice suits this
question, not a textbook definition:

- **Design**: cross-sectional, longitudinal, experimental, quasi-experimental,
  case study, mixed methods.
- **Study area and population**, and why them.
- **Sampling**: the frame, the technique (simple random, stratified, systematic,
  purposive, snowball), and the **sample size with its calculation shown**. A
  size asserted without a calculation is the most common single reason a
  proposal comes back.
- **Instruments**: questionnaire, interview guide, observation checklist,
  secondary data. How were they validated? Was there a pilot?
- **Variables**: how each is operationalised and measured. "Poverty" is not a
  variable; "monthly household expenditure per adult equivalent" is.
- **Analysis plan**: name the specific tests, chosen before data collection.
  See the `statistical-test-choice` skill.
- **Limitations**: state them yourself. A panel that finds a limitation you
  missed trusts nothing else in the document.

## Ethics — not a formality

Informed consent (and assent plus guardian consent for minors), voluntary
participation and the right to withdraw, confidentiality and how data will be
stored, anonymisation, any risk to participants and how it is mitigated, and the
committee the proposal will be submitted to. For Tanzanian research, name the
relevant institutional review board and any national clearance required.

## Work plan and budget

A Gantt-style table by month, and a costed budget with unit costs. If the budget
cannot fund the sample size, the sample size is wrong — fix it now rather than
in the field.

## How to help

Draft WITH the student. Ask them to supply the question, the setting and the
constraints; you supply structure, method options with trade-offs, and hard
questions about feasibility. Push back where the method cannot reach the
question — that is the most valuable thing you can do here, and it is what a
good supervisor would do.
